Common Solar Service Problems and How to Fix Them

Feb, 05 2026

Solar energy systems are engineered for long-term performance. However, like any technology-driven solution, they may occasionally experience service-related issues. These challenges are not necessarily indicators of poor product quality; instead, they often arise due to environmental conditions, installation practices, usage patterns, or delayed maintenance. 

For both residential and commercial users, timely identification and resolution of service issues is essential to maintaining system efficiency, reliability, and return on investment. Understanding common service problems and knowing how to address them helps minimize downtime, prevent avoidable losses, and ensure uninterrupted system operation. 

In this blog, we outline the most common service issues encountered in solar and energy storage systems, their causes, practical corrective actions, and how a strong after-sales support framework ensures efficient resolution.

Why Service issues occur in Solar Systems?

Solar power systems have been designed for reliable operation over many years. However, they function in real-world environments where external factors can influence performance. 

Dust accumulation, high ambient temperatures, humidity, and grid voltage fluctuations can impact components such as inverters, batteries, and communication modules. Changes in energy consumption patterns, shading conditions, or internet connectivity may also affect system output. In some cases, installation or commissioning parameters may not be fully optimized, contributing to operational challenges. 

The positive reality is that most solar service issues are predictable and manageable. When identified early through monitoring, regular inspections, or timely service intervention, they can be resolved quickly without affecting system efficiency or long-term returns.

Common Solar Service Problems and How to fix them? 

1. Reduced Solar Power Generation 

Symptoms: Daily energy production drops significantly compared to historical averages, despite similar weather conditions. 

Common Causes: 

  1. Accumulated dust or dirt on panels reducing light transmission by 15–25%

  2. Shading from new construction, tree growth, or debris

  3. Normal panel degradation (0.5–0.8% annually), accelerated by defects

  4. Inverter efficiency loss or MPPT malfunction

  5. Degraded wiring connections increasing electrical resistance 

Basic Corrective Actions: 

  1. Clean solar panels using soft brushes or squeegees with plain water

  2. Inspect the site throughout the day for new shading sources

  3. Review monitoring data and compare current output with historical performance 

When to Contact Service Support: If generation drops exceed 15% after cleaning, if visible panel damage is present, if entire panel strings are non-functional, or if electrical connection issues are suspected, professional inspection is recommended. 

2. Inverter Not Powering On 

Symptoms: The inverter display remains blank; indicator lights are off, and the system produces no output despite adequate sunlight. 

Common Causes: 

  1. Tripped circuit breaker or blown fuse on the DC or AC side

  2. Loose or disconnected wiring

  3. Insufficient DC voltage during early morning or late evening hours

  4. Grid voltage outside permissible limits

  5. Internal inverter component failure 

Basic Corrective Actions: 

  1. Check DC and AC circuit breakers and ensure they are in the ON position

  2. Inspect visible wiring connections for looseness or corrosion

  3. Verify that solar panels are generating sufficient DC voltage using the monitoring system 

When to Contact Service Support: If breakers trip repeatedly, visible component damage is observed, the inverter fails to start despite adequate input voltage, or if electrical troubleshooting is not safe to perform, professional service support should be contacted immediately. 

3. Battery Not Charging 

Symptoms: Battery voltage remains low despite sufficient solar generation or grid availability. 

Common Causes: 

  1. Blown fuse in the charging circuit

  2. Loose or corroded battery terminals

  3. Reverse polarity connections

  4. Charge controller malfunction

  5. Deep discharge triggering battery protection mode 

Basic Corrective Actions: 

  1. Inspect and clean battery terminals using appropriate safety procedures

  2. Check and replace blown fuses with identical ratings

  3. Verify correct polarity using a multimeter 

When to Contact Service Support: If charging does not resume after basic checks, if controller failure is suspected, or if batteries are deeply discharged beyond recovery thresholds, professional service intervention is required. 

4. Monitoring App or Wi-Fi Connectivity Issues 

Symptoms: The monitoring application displays offline status or fails to show real-time data, while the inverter continues operating normally. 

Basic Corrective Actions: 

  1. Restart the inverter to reset the communication module

  2. Check Wi-Fi signal strength at the inverter location

  3. Verify router settings and confirm password accuracy

  4. Update the monitoring application to the latest version

When to Contact Service Support: Persistent connectivity issues, damaged communication modules, or reconfiguration requirements should be handled by technical support teams.

Solving Problems before they occur

Preventive maintenance significantly reduces the likelihood of service issues and improves long-term system performance. 

Recommended practices include: 

  1. Quarterly visual inspections of inverters and batteries

  2. Semi-annual panel cleaning (more frequently in dusty areas)

  3. Monthly electrolyte checks for lead-acid batteries

  4. Weekly monitoring data review to establish performance baselines

  5. Annual professional system inspections

  6. Regular firmware updates for inverters and monitoring devices
