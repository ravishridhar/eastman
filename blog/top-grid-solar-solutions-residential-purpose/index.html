Understanding On-Grid Solar Solutions

To make an informed decision, it is essential to understand exactly what an on-grid solar system is and how it functions within a residential setting. An on-grid solar system, also called a grid-tied or grid-connected solar setup, connects your rooftop solar panels directly to the public electricity grid. The system generates electricity from sunlight and powers your home, while excess energy can be exported to the grid through net metering, reducing your electricity bill further. 

Unlike off-grid systems, on-grid solar setups don’t require batteries to function during the night or on cloudy days. On-grid systems utilize the utility grid as a virtual battery, making them more cost-effective, easy to install, and simpler to maintain. 

How On-Grid Systems Work: The process is straightforward and efficient as follows- 

1. Generation: Solar panels installed on your roof capture sunlight and convert it into Direct Current electricity. 

2. Conversion: This DC electricity flows into a solar inverter, which converts it into Alternating Current, the standard form of electricity used to power home appliances. 

3. Consumption: The home uses solar-generated power first. 

4. Export: If the system generates more power than the home needs, excess electricity is automatically exported to the utility grid. 

5. Import: When solar panels are not generating power, such as at night, the home draws electricity from the grid as usual. 

 

Components of an On-Grid Solar System

A standard residential on-grid system is composed of several key components, each playing a critical role in energy generation and management. To build an efficient on-grid solar system, these key elements work in harmony: 

1. Solar Photovoltaic (PV) Panels: These are the primary energy collectors. High-efficiency monocrystalline or polycrystalline panels are typically used to capture sunlight and convert it to electricity to maximize energy production even within limited roof space. 

 

2. Solar Inverter: The heart of the system. In an on-grid setup, the inverter ensures the electricity produced matches the voltage and frequency of the grid, as it converts DC power from panels to AC power for home use.  

 

3. Net Meter: This is a bi-directional meter that replaces your standard electricity meter. It tracks both the power you draw from the grid and the power you export to it. This allows "net metering," in which you are credited for the excess energy you produce. 

4. Mounting Structure: A sturdy framework that secures the panels to the roof, ensuring they are installed safely and oriented at the optimal angle to capture sunlight. 

 

5. Balance of System: This includes cables, wiring, junction boxes, and safety switches required to connect the components safely. Ensure a safe and robust connection, safety components such as DC/AC isolators, surge protection devices, and high-quality wiring ensure system safety and longevity in your residence and grid.

Why On-Grid Solar is Ideal for Residential Use

1. Significant Reduction in Electricity Bills: A well-designed residential on-grid solar system can reduce electricity bills by 70–90% depending on system size and usage patterns. During the day, the home runs on free solar power. Any excess exported to the grid generates credits, which can be used to pay for the electricity consumed at night. Over time, the system pays itself, making solar a long-term financial asset rather than an expense for residential use.  

2. Net Metering Benefits: Net Metering is a game-changer for residential purposes. When your solar system generates more power than you consume, the surplus electricity is exported to the grid, and you receive credits. These credits offset future electricity consumption, especially during monsoon or winter months when solar generation is lower. 

3. Environmentally Responsible Energy: Adopting solar energy is a powerful way to contribute to a greener planet. By generating clean, renewable energy at home, you directly reduce the demand for fossil fuel-based power plants. This leads to a significant decrease in carbon footprints. For the eco-conscious homeowner, this provides a sense of contribution to community health and the well-being of future generations. 

4. Low Maintenance & High Reliability: Since on-grid systems do not use batteries, maintenance requirements are minimal. Regular panel cleaning and periodic inspections are sufficient to keep the system running efficiently for 25+ years. High-quality components from trusted brands like Eastman ensure durability, safety, and consistent performance. 

5. Increased Property Value: Homes equipped with rooftop solar systems are seen as modern, future-ready properties. Buyers increasingly value energy-efficient homes, making solar-powered houses more attractive in the real estate market. 

How to Install an On-Grid Solar System

A successful transition to solar energy requires proper installation and routine care. While technical in nature, the process is streamlined when handled by professionals. Steps for installation are as follows: 

1. Site Assessment: A solar engineer visits the property to analyze the roof structure, shadow-free area, and orientation. They also review past electricity bills to determine the necessary system capacity (e.g., 3kW, 5kW, or 10kW). 

2. System Design: Based on the assessment, a customized layout is created to maximize sunlight exposure. 

3. Permits and Approvals: Documentation is submitted to the local utility company and relevant government agencies to approve the grid connection and net metering. 

4.Installation: The mounting structure is fixed to the roof, followed by the panels and the inverter. Wiring is routed safely to the main distribution board. 

5.Commissioning: Once the utility company installs the bi-directional net meter, the system is tested and officially switched on.

FAQ’s

Q. Does an on-grid solar system work during a power outage? 

A. No. Standard on-grid systems are designed to shut down immediately during a grid failure. This is a safety feature called "anti-islanding" to protect utility workers from fixing the lines from being electrocuted by the power your panels generate. 

Q. Can I run my air conditioner on solar power? 

A. Yes. On-grid systems are synchronized with the grid. You can run heavy loads like ACs. If your solar generation is sufficient at that moment, it runs on solar. If not, the system seamlessly draws the extra required power from the grid. 

Q. How much maintenance does a solar system require? 

A. Very little. Cleaning the panels with water every few weeks to remove dust is usually sufficient. There are no moving parts to wear out. 

Q. What happens to the excess electricity I generate? 

A. Excess electricity is exported to the government grid. Your bi-directional net meter records this, and your utility provider gives you credit for these units, which is adjusted against your electricity bill. 

Q. Which on-grid solar system is best for homes in India? 

A. A standard rooftop on-grid solar system with net metering is best for most Indian homes due to affordability and high savings.
